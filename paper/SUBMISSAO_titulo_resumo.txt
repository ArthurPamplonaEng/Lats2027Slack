TITLE
How Far from Failure Is a Zero Slack Reading? Predicting and Guard-Banding the Offset of an FPGA Aging Sensor

ABSTRACT (199 words)
Silicon lifecycle management (SLM) acts on phase-swept slack sensors, yet whether a zero reading marks the functional failure of the monitored path is rarely measured. We drive a tapped delay chain on a 16 nm Artix UltraScale+ FPGA through failure by lowering the core voltage, each tap carrying the sensor and a functional checker on one capture flip-flop. On 81 failing taps in four placements, the zero reading and the first error were −462 to +404 ps of slack apart (negative: error first). This offset is the sensor’s own sampling skew: without the analysis-only margins of its cross-clock path, static timing predicts it to 77 ps rms (175 ps for a constant), and to 70 ps on two placements predicted before measurement. A static phase on the system clock turns it into a designed guard band without moving the failure voltage; sized from static timing, it would cover every measured tap. On a 28 nm Artix-7, a burn-in’s slack loss on an adder path at three core voltages amounts to one supply shift of 14.3 mV. A zero reading is a failure warning only once its offset is known: static timing predicts it, and one voltage sweep measures it.

KEYWORDS
Aging sensor, timing margin, FPGA, functional failure, guard band, burn-in, silicon lifecycle management
